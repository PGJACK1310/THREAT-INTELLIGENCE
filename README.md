# THREAT-INTELLIGENCE
Threat Intelligence (TI) is evidence-based knowledge about existing or emerging threats that helps organizations make informed security decisions. It answers three questions: Who is attacking? How are they attacking? Why are they attacking?
- Threat Intelligence is evidence-based knowledge about existing or emerging threats — including context, mechanisms, indicators, and actionable advice — that helps you make faster, smarter security decisions.

1.Core Goal
Know the enemy before attack
2.Key Output
Actionable, timely decisions
3.Primary Benefit
Reduce detection time

## Raw Data vs Information vs Intelligence

| Layer        | Example                                                                    | Who Uses It     |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Raw Data     | IP address `185.220.101.47` seen in logs                                   | Machines / SIEM |
| Information  | That IP is a Tor exit node                                                 | Analysts        |
| Intelligence | Tor exit node used by APT29 to exfiltrate HR data — block + hunt laterally | Decision makers |

### Why Threat Intelligence Matters

- Proactive Defense
Instead of reacting after a breach, TI lets you predict and prevent attacks before they happen by understanding attacker behavior patterns.
- Faster Response
When an incident occurs, pre-built threat profiles reduce Mean Time to Detect (MTTD) and Mean Time to Respond (MTTR) significantly.
- Prioritize Resources
Not every threat applies to you. TI helps security teams focus energy on threats relevant to your industry, geography, and technology stack.
- Executive Reporting
Strategic intelligence translates technical risk into business language — helping boards understand cybersecurity investment decisions.
