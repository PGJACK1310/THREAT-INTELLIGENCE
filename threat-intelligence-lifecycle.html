CYBERSECURITY FRAMEWORK

Threat Intelligence Life Cycle

A complete operational guide to collecting, analyzing, and actioning cyber threat intelligence

Threat Intelligence is evidence-based knowledge — including context, mechanisms, indicators, implications, and actionable advice — about an existing or emerging menace or hazard to assets. The Threat Intelligence Life Cycle is a structured, continuous process that organizations use to plan, collect, process, analyze, and disseminate intelligence so that decision-makers can act with speed, accuracy, and confidence. Unlike ad-hoc vulnerability scanning, a mature TI programme turns raw data into contextually rich, operationally relevant insights that drive proactive defense.

DEEP DIVE

Phase-by-Phase Breakdown

01
🎯
Phase 01 Planning & Direction

The foundation of every intelligence programme. Stakeholders define Intelligence Requirements (IRs) — the critical questions that, if answered, would reduce risk or improve decision-making. Without clear direction, collection becomes unfocused and analysis wastes resources.

  • Define Priority Intelligence Requirements (PIRs) aligned to business risk
  • Identify relevant threat actors, sectors, and geographies
  • Establish scope: strategic, operational, or tactical focus
  • Allocate resources: analysts, tooling, feeds, budget
  • Set classification policies and sharing agreements (TLP)
  • Define cadence, SLAs, and output formats for stakeholders
02
📡
Phase 02 Collection

Systematic gathering of raw data from diverse sources mapped to IRs. Collection must be broad enough to avoid blind spots, yet targeted enough to remain manageable. Data quality at this stage directly determines analysis quality downstream.

  • OSINT — open-source: news, blogs, social media, pastebin, CVE databases
  • TECHINT — network logs, endpoint telemetry, SIEM events, honeypots
  • HUMINT — threat researcher interviews, dark-web forums, vendor briefings
  • FININT — cryptocurrency wallet tracking, financial threat indicators
  • Commercial threat feeds (STIX/TAXII), ISAC sharing communities
  • Active collection: sandboxes, malware detonation, threat hunting
03
⚙️
Phase 03 Processing

Raw data is transformed into a usable format. This involves normalization, deduplication, translation, enrichment, and correlation. Processing is often automated via SIEM, SOAR, or TIP platforms but requires analyst oversight for quality.

  • Normalize data formats: JSON, STIX 2.1, CSV, XML, MISP
  • Enrich IoCs with whois, GeoIP, passive DNS, VirusTotal, Shodan
  • Deduplicate and remove false positives or stale indicators
  • Correlate logs and events to identify patterns or kill-chain stages
  • Tag and classify data by threat actor, campaign, and tactic (MITRE ATT&CK)
  • Ingest into Threat Intelligence Platform (TIP) or SIEM for further analysis
04
🔬
Phase 04 Analysis

The analytical heart of the cycle. Processed data is examined to produce actionable intelligence. Analysts apply structured analytic techniques, adversary models, and kill-chain mapping to answer stakeholder intelligence requirements.

  • Apply MITRE ATT&CK framework: map TTPs to enterprise matrix
  • Use Diamond Model and Lockheed Kill Chain for adversary profiling
  • Conduct threat actor attribution and campaign tracking
  • Assess likelihood, impact, and confidence using structured language
  • Identify gaps, novel techniques, and emerging malware families
  • Produce analytic products: threat assessments, BLUF reports, RFIs
05
📤
Phase 05 Dissemination

Intelligence is delivered to the right people, at the right time, in the right format. Dissemination failures — wrong audience, wrong format, too late — render perfect analysis worthless. Tailor every product to its consumer.

  • Apply Traffic Light Protocol (TLP) classifications for controlled sharing
  • Automate IoC feeds via TAXII to firewalls, SIEM, EDR, SOAR
  • Produce executive briefings: concise BLUF, risk-focused language
  • Share tactical alerts with SOC: enriched alerts, YARA/Snort rules
  • Publish structured reports in STIX/MISP for partner organizations
  • Use ISAC channels and government portals (CISA, NCSC) for sector sharing
06
🔄
Phase 06 Feedback & Review

Intelligence is only as good as its ability to evolve. Feedback closes the loop — stakeholders assess whether intelligence answered their requirements, and the programme refines its direction, collection sources, and analysis methods accordingly.

  • Gather consumer feedback: was the intelligence actionable and timely?
  • Measure KPIs: detection rate, mean-time-to-detect (MTTD), IoC accuracy
  • Review PIRs: do they still reflect current business risk?
  • Evaluate source quality: high-fidelity vs noisy feeds
  • Adjust collection priorities based on missed threats or coverage gaps
  • Document lessons learned; refine analytic tradecraft and SOPs

CLASSIFICATION

Types of Threat Intelligence

🌐
Strategic

High-level trends, geopolitical risk, long-term threat landscape analysis. Non-technical, risk-oriented language suitable for business leaders and boards.

C-Suite · Board
⚔️
Tactical

Adversary TTPs, malware techniques, MITRE ATT&CK mappings. Helps security teams understand how attackers operate and fortify defences accordingly.

Security Teams · Architects
🗺️
Operational

Details about specific planned or ongoing attacks — actor campaigns, target industries, timing, and infrastructure used. Drives proactive hunting and IR readiness.

SOC · Incident Responders
🔩
Technical

Machine-readable IoCs: IP addresses, domains, file hashes, URLs, email headers. Consumed directly by security tools for automated blocking and detection.

SIEM · EDR · Firewalls

FRAMEWORKS & MODELS

Key Analytical Frameworks

⚔️
MITRE ATT&CKTactics, Techniques & Procedures

A globally accessible knowledge base of adversary TTPs based on real-world observations. Used to map intelligence findings, identify detection gaps, and measure defensive coverage across 14 tactic categories and hundreds of techniques.

🔗
Lockheed Kill ChainIntrusion Kill Chain Model

Models an attacker's campaign as a sequential chain: Reconnaissance → Weaponization → Delivery → Exploitation → Installation → C2 → Actions on Objectives. Intelligence helps defenders deny attackers at each stage before mission success.

💎
Diamond ModelIntrusion Analysis Framework

Structures intelligence around four core features: Adversary, Infrastructure, Capability, and Victim. The model enables analysts to pivot between features to uncover related activity, campaigns, and actor attribution with structured confidence.

🔺
Pyramid of PainIoC Value Framework

Illustrates the relative value of different IoC types — from trivial-to-change hash values at the base to impossible-to-change TTPs at the apex. High-tier intelligence (TTPs) causes maximum disruption to adversary operations when acted upon.

🚦
TLP ProtocolTraffic Light Protocol

A standardized framework for controlling intelligence sharing. Four colours govern distribution: RED (personal only), AMBER (limited), GREEN (community), WHITE (public). Ensures sensitive intelligence reaches only authorized parties.

📦
STIX / TAXIIStructured Sharing Standards

STIX (Structured Threat Information eXpression) provides a standardized language for representing cyber threat intelligence. TAXII (Trusted Automated eXchange) is the transport protocol. Together they enable machine-readable, automated intelligence sharing at scale.

OPERATIONAL EXCELLENCE

Best Practices for a Mature TI Programme

01
Requirements-Driven Collection

Never collect without a requirement. Every feed, source, and hunt must trace back to a documented intelligence requirement tied to organisational risk.

02
Automate Ruthlessly

Automate ingestion, enrichment, and IoC distribution to reduce analyst fatigue and increase velocity. Reserve human judgment for high-value analytic tasks.

03
Context Over Volume

A thousand uncontextualised IPs are worthless. One well-attributed, time-bounded, confidence-scored IoC with campaign context is invaluable. Prioritise quality.

04
Share & Collaborate

Join sector ISACs, government sharing portals, and trusted peer communities. Threat intelligence is a force-multiplier; collective defence scales better than siloed programs.

05
Integrate ATT&CK Mapping

Map every intelligence finding to MITRE ATT&CK techniques. This creates a common analytic language across teams and directly informs detection engineering priorities.

06
Measure & Improve

Track KPIs: IoC accuracy rate, time-from-collection-to-action, analyst coverage hours, and detection uplift. Use metrics to make a data-driven case for investment.

07
Tailor to Your Audience

An executive report and a YARA rule serve different consumers. Tailor format, classification level, and technical depth to each audience — never produce one-size-fits-all products.

08
Lifecycle is Continuous

The threat landscape never stops evolving. Treat the intelligence cycle as a perpetual loop, not a project with an end date. Regular review of PIRs is non-negotiable.
